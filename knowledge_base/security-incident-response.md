# Security Incident Response

Owner: Security Team. This procedure explains how to report and handle security incidents.

## What Counts as an Incident

A security incident is any event that could compromise the confidentiality, integrity or availability of Kestrel Systems systems or data. Examples include malware infections, unauthorised access, data sent to the wrong recipient, lost devices and suspected account compromise.

## Reporting

Anyone who suspects an incident must report it immediately in the security-incidents channel or by emailing security@kestrel.example. Out of hours, call the security on-call number listed on the intranet. Do not try to investigate the incident yourself or delete any evidence.

## Severity Levels

- SEV1: active compromise of production systems or confirmed exposure of Restricted data. The on-call engineer must acknowledge the page within 15 minutes, and the Chief Information Security Officer is informed immediately.
- SEV2: a contained compromise or a suspected exposure of Confidential data. Acknowledgement is required within 30 minutes.
- SEV3: a policy violation or a vulnerability with limited impact. Handled within one working day.
- SEV4: a low-risk event, such as a reported phishing email that nobody interacted with. Handled within five working days.

## Response Steps

The incident lead contains the incident, preserves evidence, removes the cause and restores normal service. Status updates for SEV1 incidents are posted every hour until the incident is resolved.

## Notification

Where personal data may have been exposed, the Legal team decides whether regulators or customers must be notified. Regulatory notification, when required, is made within 72 hours of the company becoming aware of the breach.

## After the Incident

A blameless post-incident review is completed within five working days of resolving every SEV1 and SEV2 incident. Actions from the review are tracked until they are closed.
